Applying engineering approaches at the molecular and cellular level to develop tools for studying human biology and disease.

Ether Dharmesh is an MD/PhD student in the Medical Scientist Training Program at the University of Pennsylvania's Perelman School of Medicine, with a background in biomedical engineering and research experience spanning biomaterials, drug delivery, and immunology. As a physician-scientist in training, her goal is to connect technology development with fundamental biomedical discovery and translate those insights into new therapeutic approaches.

Research

Engineering at the molecular and cellular level

RotationHaj Dezfulian lab · Penn · 2026

Engineering platforms for immunological and therapeutic applications

Currently rotating in the Haj Dezfulian laboratory, applying engineering platforms to immunological and therapeutic applications.

Co-first authorZustiak lab · Saint Louis University · 2020–2024

Microspheres that decide when their cargo leaves

Nanosilicates added to PEG hydrogel microspheres slow small-molecule release; aqueous two-phase separation keeps the particles injectable. Two further projects: super-lubricious microgels for knee osteoarthritis, and radiopaque microspheres for prostate embolization.

Frontiers in Biomaterials Science, 2023

2nd authorRevzin lab · Mayo Clinic · 2022

Magnetic capsules that hold living cells

Human stem-cell spheroids encapsulated with magnetic nanoparticles by microfluidics — cells you can sustain, then move and retrieve with a magnet. For targeted regeneration and cryopreservation.

Microsystems & Nanoengineering, 2024

Summer 2023Tsourkas lab · Penn Radiation Oncology

Getting immunotherapy to reach the tumor

PEG-PCL micelles carrying a VEGFR inhibitor, tested for whether normalizing tumor vasculature first changes the response to subsequent anti-CTLA-4 treatment. The project that connected delivery materials to immunotherapy.
